# 2. How long you have

**There is no statutory maximum retention in BiH and no 30-day default.** Do not
let anyone tell you otherwise. Retention is governed only by the
storage-limitation principle in **Art. 7(1)(e)** and by the controller's own
decision. The "at least 12 months" in **Art. 57(6)** applies to *access logs*,
not footage — a trap that catches a lot of secondary commentary.

EDPB Guidelines 3/2019 §121, which the BiH Agency applies in practice, says
footage should in most cases be erased **after a few days**, and that anything
**beyond 72 hours** requires positive justification. Registered BiH controllers
show retention periods like *"21 dan"*.

**So the realistic window is 24 hours to a few weeks, and you cannot know which.
Send the preservation request today.**

# 3. Strategy: three documents, same day, one packet

File all three as **separately numbered documents in one packet**, each
expressly reserving the others, so the body cannot treat answering one as
disposing of the rest.

| | ZZLP Art. 17 (access) | FBiH freedom of information |
|---|---|---|
| Deadline | 30 days (+60 extension) | **15 days** |
| Third-party bar | Art. 17(4), "rights and freedoms of others" | Art. 8 protects a **third person** — not your own data |
| Redaction | EDPB guidance, persuasive only | **Art. 10 severance, statutory** |
| Forces a formal decision | No | **Yes — a *rješenje* with appeal instructions** |
| Enforcement teeth | **Strong** — Agency can order production | Weaker (administrative appeal, Ombudsman) |
| Works if controller is private | **Yes** | No |

**Order:** (1) preservation request — the only genuinely time-critical item;
(2) the FOI request — fastest clock and forces an appealable decision; (3) the
Art. 17 request — the only track that lets the Agency *order* production, and
the only one that still works if the camera turns out to belong to a contracted
security firm rather than the institution.

Filing both statutes is not counterproductive and each says so: **Art. 108(1)**
preserves other remedies expressly, and FBiH FOI **Art. 4** is a freestanding
right.

## Two things that decide the outcome

**BOTH OF YOU MUST SIGN EVERY DOCUMENT, AND EACH MUST CONSENT TO THE OTHER
APPEARING.** You are two people, so relative to each other you are each a
"third party" — which is the controller's only real ground for refusal under
Art. 17(4). A joint request in which each expressly consents to the other
receiving footage showing you both removes that objection entirely. This is the
single highest-value move available to you and it is built into the drafts below.

**DO NOT ALLEGE THE CAMERA IS UNLAWFUL, and do not ask the Agency to assess its
lawfulness.** In published practice the standard remedy for unlawful
surveillance is a ban **plus an order to delete the recordings**. Arguing
illegality is the fastest way to have your footage destroyed by regulatory
order. Ask only for access.

# 5. Your legal position in detail

**The law:** Zakon o zaštiti ličnih podataka, "Službeni glasnik BiH" br. 12/25 —
published 28.02.2025, in force 08.03.2025, applicable from **04.10.2025** (Art.
120). Art. 119 repealed the 2006 law and its bylaws. GDPR-aligned; BiH numbering
runs **GDPR + 2** in Part Two (GDPR 15 → BiH 17).

- **Art. 17(3)** — the controller "osigurava kopiju ličnog podatka koji se
  obrađuje". A reasonable fee only for *additional* copies.
- **Art. 14(3)** — respond without undue delay and **within 30 days**, extendable
  by **60 days** with notice of the extension inside the first 30.
- **Art. 14(4)** — if it does not act, it must give reasons within 30 days and
  inform you of the right to complain to the Agency or sue.
- **Art. 14(5)–(6)** — free of charge; a fee or refusal only if the request is
  "očito neosnovan ili pretjeran", and **the burden of proving that is on the
  controller**.
- **Art. 13(2)** — if the controller genuinely cannot identify you, Arts. 17–22
  don't apply *unless* you supply identifying information. Hence stating the
  exact time and spot.
- **Art. 20(1)(c) and (2)** — restriction of processing where you need the data
  to establish or exercise legal claims; once restricted, the data "smije se
  obrađivati samo uz saglasnost nosioca podataka, **izuzev čuvanja**" — storage
  is mandated, deletion is not permitted. This is the preservation hook. It is
  arguable rather than certain, but it is the correct citation.
- **Art. 57(5)** — the notice must state that the area is monitored, who the
  controller or processor is, **and contact details for exercising your rights**,
  visible "najkasnije prilikom ulaska u vidokrug snimanja".
- **Art. 57(4)** — the controller must adopt a video-surveillance decision
  stating the area, purpose, number of cameras, what each covers, **the retention
  period**, and how data subjects exercise their rights. Asking for a copy of
  that decision is a cheap, strong first move: it discloses the retention period
  and the access procedure.
- **Art. 37(3)(c)** — a DPIA is mandatory for systematic large-scale monitoring
  of publicly accessible areas. Leverage, not a route to the footage.
- **Art. 108(1)** — right to complain to the Agency, expressly "ne dovodeći u
  pitanje druga upravna ili sudska pravna sredstva".

**Form:** none required. The Agency's own citizens' guidance says an access
request is submitted to the controller **"u slobodnoj formi"**. Registered post
is not legally required either — but it is what proves the date of receipt, which
is what starts every clock, and published Agency case files turn on exactly that
proof. Use it.

**Fines — corrected for a public body.** **Art. 113(10)** exempts public
authorities themselves from fines; only the responsible or employed person can be
fined, **5,000–70,000 KM** (Art. 113(8), whose list includes Art. 57). The much
larger Art. 113(5)(b) range applies to private controllers, not to this one.

**Expect this argument:** **Art. 116(2)** gives controllers **two years from
entry into force — until 8 March 2027** — to bring existing processing into
compliance. It explains why signage may be missing. It does **not** suspend your
Arts. 14–24 rights, which have applied since 4 October 2025.

**If they claim a law-enforcement purpose,** the regime shifts to Part Three,
where access is Art. 70 subject to Art. 71 restrictions — materially weaker. On
these facts (site protection, not policing) Art. 17 governs. Art. 57(8) requires
monitoring of public space for law-enforcement purposes to be authorised by a
specific law — if they invoke it, ask them to name that law.

## The registration register is a dead end

The registration duty was **abolished** when the new law became applicable. The
Agency's own notice states that from 04.10.2025 it no longer maintains the
Glavni registar. Under the new law the duty is internal record-keeping only —
**Art. 32**, disclosed to the Agency on request under Art. 32(4), not published.

The old register is still searchable at **https://azlpreg.azlp.ba/Pretraga**
(single "Pojam" field, matching controller or collection name only — **no search
by address or location**; data frozen around 2022). It was searched for *Blagaj*,
*Mostar*, *Grad Mostar*, *Stari grad*, *Stjepan*, *spomenik*, *baštin*, *zavod za
zaštitu*, *turiz* — **no video-surveillance entry for any Blagaj or Mostar
heritage custodian**. Worth two minutes for completeness, but it will not
identify this camera.

## What will identify the controller, fastest first

1. **The sign on site.** Art. 57(5) requires it to name the controller *and*
   give contact details for exercising rights. Photograph it — and if there is
   no sign, photograph its absence, dated. In published practice the **controller
   bears the burden of proving compliant signage**, and a security company's
   report was rejected as insufficient.
2. **Ask on site** — custodian, staff, anyone at the entrance.
3. **The FOI request above**, item 3 — a fast, low-friction way to force
   identification even before the footage question is decided.
4. **The Art. 32 records**, obtainable only through the Agency under Art. 32(4).

  https://azlp.ba/Gradani/?id=4717). Five sections: your details; representative;
  the controller complained of; description of the breach with evidence; and
  **whether you approached the controller first** — so attach the correspondence.
  Do not file before approaching the controller; it will be bounced.
- **How to file, from the form itself:** by post; **in person at the Agency**;
  scanned by email to **azlpinfo@azlp.ba**; or **orally, on the record, at the
  Agency's offices**.
- **No fee.**
- **Agency deadline: 90 days** (Art. 109(2)); past that, or without notice of
  progress, you may bring an *upravni spor* before the Court of BiH.
- **Realistic handling time: 7–9 months**, judging by a published case file
  (complaint August 2020 → decision April 2021). Plan accordingly.
- **Appeal:** no administrative appeal — *upravni spor* before the Court of BiH
  within 60 days (Arts. 103(4), 109(1)). Art. 110 allows an independent civil
  action; Art. 112 damages.
- **The Ombudsman** is available on the public-body theory via the FBiH FOI route,
  as a free parallel avenue needing no lawyer.

**The fastest binding preservation tool — court.** *Osiguranje dokaza* under
**ZPP FBiH Arts. 169–173**, available where there is justified fear evidence will
be lost, **including before litigation starts** (Art. 169(1)). Jurisdiction:
**Općinski sud u Mostaru** (Art. 170(2), the court where the thing to be
inspected is). The court may exceptionally begin taking evidence **before** the
ruling is served on the opponent, with no appeal against that (Art. 172(6)–(7)).
Needs a local lawyer and a day or two — worth it only if the Agency stonewalls
while the loop is still alive.

**What actually works in practice:** a published Agency case records a written
request to a controller to preserve footage — *"da potrebne snimke video nadzora
sačuva"* — resulting in the recordings being *"izdvojeni i sačuvani po
zahtjevu"*, which the Agency treated as legitimate. Which is why Document 1 goes
out today.

**Honest uncertainty, the biggest in this analysis:** no published Agency
decision or opinion ordering footage handed to a data subject could be found.
The Art. 17(3) right to a copy is clear on the face of the statute but appears
untested in published BiH practice. The one closely analogous decision ordered
*information about processing*, not a copy.
