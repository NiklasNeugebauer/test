# Requesting the Stjepan Grad footage — 9 September 2026, ~07:00

## Who operates the camera

**Agencija "Stari grad" Mostar** — a City of Mostar public institution — is the
near-certain operator.

The documentary trail: in 2016 the Agency carried out the project
*"Iluminacija Starog grada Blagaj (utvrda Herceg Stjepan grada)"*, under which
it installed 15 LED floodlights above the walls, **an anti-intrusion system, and
video surveillance to protect the floodlights** ("protivprovalni sistem, kao i
videonadzor za zaštitu reflektora").

- Contractor: **Amitea d.o.o. Mostar** (33,797.79 KM incl. VAT)
- Supervision: IGH d.o.o. Mostar
- Additional works contract 26.12.2016 (4,526.64 KM) itemised as
  "zaštita-video nadzor, zaštita-protivprovala" plus commissioning an **ADSL**
  link. Works completed 28.12.2016; acceptance 30.12.2016.
- Funders: Federalno ministarstvo raseljenih osoba i izbjeglica (30,000 KM),
  Ministarstvo civilnih poslova BiH (5,000 KM), Federalno ministarstvo kulture
  i sporta (4,526 KM)

The Agency has run the site ever since — phase IV wall conservation (2021),
routine Blagaj maintenance, and an April–December 2026 technical-protection
monitoring arrangement with an operational centre and mobile intervention teams.

### Why this matches the hardware seen on site

- Mains power exists on those towers *only* because of the 2016 lighting scheme
  — which is what makes a motorised PTZ dome possible up there at all.
- The 2016 uplink was ADSL, implausible to sustain on a 310 m karst summit. A
  white box with signal-strength LEDs (LTE/4G CPE) is exactly what replaces a
  failed ADSL line. The uplink changed; the installation didn't.
- Purpose of record — protecting the floodlights, plus anti-intrusion — matches
  a dome aimed *inward* at the courtyard and the visitor entrance.

**Not confirmed:** no document or news report specifically describes a PTZ dome
or any post-2016 upgrade. That the current camera is a PTZ on LTE is inference
from the hardware matching an upgraded uplink on an existing powered install.

## Your legal position

Governing law: **Zakon o zaštiti ličnih podataka BiH, "Službeni glasnik BiH"
br. 12/25** — published 28.02.2025, in force 08.03.2025, applicable from
04.10.2025. GDPR-aligned.

- **Član 17** — right of access, including "kopiju ličnog podatka koji se
  obrađuje". Controller must respond **without undue delay and within 30 days**,
  extendable by 60 days if you are told why inside the first 30. **Free of
  charge** (fees only for additional copies or manifestly excessive requests).
  Refusal must state reasons and advise of your right to complain or sue.
- **Član 17(4)** — the copy "ne smije negativno uticati na prava i slobode
  drugih". Expect other visitors blurred, or a still instead of video. Your
  being alone up there removes this objection, so say so.
- **Član 57(5)** — the surveillance notice must be displayed and must state
  **(a)** that the area is under surveillance, **(b)** who the controller or
  processor is, and **(c)** contact details for exercising your rights — and be
  visible **before you enter the camera's field of view**.
- **Član 108** — complaint to AZLP. Breach of the signage duty carries fines of
  **10,000–100,000 KM** for a legal person. So a *missing* sign is not a dead
  end — it is leverage, and grounds for a complaint that would compel
  identification.
- The law's household exemption (private individual, personal use) does **not**
  apply here, because the operator is a public institution. That is in your
  favour.

Article numbers come from a legal-database compilation of the law text. The AZLP
help desk can confirm them.
